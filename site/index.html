The value of incomplete work

We have been conditioned to believe that a finished project is the only proof of progress. We equate completion with value and leave the rest in the shadows of 'unfinished business.' But there is a specific, quiet utility in the things we don’t finish. When you stop a project, a draft, or an idea before it reaches its conventional conclusion, you aren't necessarily failing. Often, you are simply harvesting the utility you needed from it and moving on. Not every thought needs to be a book. Not every conversation needs to be a resolution. Not every ambition needs a trophy or a final signature. If you find yourself holding onto half-finished tasks, ask yourself if you are truly stuck, or if you simply extracted the insight you were looking for. Sometimes, the most professional thing you can do is recognize that a pursuit has served its purpose, even if it hasn't reached the finish line. Efficiency isn't always about finishing; sometimes, it's about knowing when to stop.

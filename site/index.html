The hidden cost of immediate clarity

We often mistake the urge for a quick decision for the need for a correct one. In professional and personal life, there is immense pressure to resolve ambiguity the moment it appears. We treat uncertainty like a leak in a boat that must be plugged immediately to avoid sinking. But some problems aren’t leaks; they are tides. They have a natural rhythm, and forcing a resolution before the full picture emerges often leads to 'solutions' that simply create a new set of complications. There is a quiet power in letting a situation sit. It isn’t about procrastination; it is about observation. When you resist the reflexive need to define the outcome too early, you gain the ability to act on the reality of the situation rather than your anxiety about it. Action is necessary, but timing is the variable that determines whether that action adds value or merely noise. Don't rush to clear the fog; sometimes you just need to wait for the light to shift.

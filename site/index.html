Calibrating decision-making speed by the cost of reversibility.

Most decision fatigue doesn’t come from the weight of our choices. It comes from treating easily reversible decisions as if they are permanent, while letting truly permanent choices happen through passive drift. Notice how easy it is to spend forty-five minutes agonizing over the wording of an internal update, comparing nearly identical productivity apps, or fine-tuning the aesthetic of a preliminary slide. If any of those choices turn out to be imperfect, the cost to correct them is almost zero. They are two-way doors. Yet the commitments that quietly shape our lives—the projects we agree to carry for the next two years, the compromises we make in our daily routines, or the professional relationships we invest our trust into—often receive very little deliberate examination. We rarely pause to evaluate them with the same intensity; we simply drift into them through momentum or convenience. When you treat every low-stakes dilemma as a crisis of precision, you deplete the cognitive energy required to make structural life decisions with clarity. Before you lose another hour to deliberation, ask a straightforward question: How difficult is it to undo this if I am wrong? If the cost of turning back is negligible, make the call quickly and learn from the friction. Save your hesitation, your deep analysis, and your sleepless nights for the doors that lock behind you.
